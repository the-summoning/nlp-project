\begin{abstract}
Visual Question Answering (VQA) requires answering natural language questions about images. We organize the VQA literature into three chronological architectural generations and empirically evaluate one representative model per generation: a CNN+LSTM baseline replicated from scratch, LXMERT, and BLIP, on VQA v2 and GQA. VQA v2 shows the expected progression (56.88\% $\rightarrow$ 70.30\% $\rightarrow$ 75.95\%), with cross-modal attention providing the largest gain. On GQA, however, the ranking inverts: LXMERT (59.29\%) outperforms BLIP (47.11\%) because open-vocabulary generation without dataset-specific fine-tuning produces wrong answers. This suggests that dataset-specific training may contribute more to cross-dataset generalization than architectural generation alone.
\end{abstract}
